Larger networks and edge electrodes under junction noise

Status report, 13 October 2026. Follows the report of 9 October (ten simulated devices per size; at a realistic junction noise level 9 of 10 devices of 2.83 mm compute beyond their input; 4 mm passed on 3 devices). This report completes 4 mm with 10 devices and compares the edge-electrode layout (split bands) with the pad array on the same tests. Simulations of random Ag nanowire networks at the working density (2000 wires of 70 µm per mm2^2), full Kirchhoff circuit with wire resistance (AgNW_FDT_Memristor_Framework), N10 volatile junctions; 690 runs, most on the CCT-Rosario HPC cluster.

Jargon in 30 seconds. A reservoir computer uses a fixed physical network as memory and trains only a linear readout. MC = memory capacity (past inputs recalled); degree-2 = nonlinear capacity; NARMA-10 = a standard task, reported as error (lower is better; input alone 0.84). G1 = "computes beyond the input": MC ≥1\ge 1, degree-2 ≥1\ge 1, NARMA ≤0.70\le 0.70, each with a 2-sd margin over all runs (pooled), or, per device, on the mean of its 2 replicates. A rupture is a random, complete break of a junction's volatile filament, at rate rr per junction; real junctions are estimated at r≈0.005–0.03r \approx 0.005\text{–}0.03 per second. Capacity kept ρ\rho = (MC + degree-2) of a run relative to the same run without ruptures.

Answer

  • 4 mm does not confirm. With 10 devices at 10−210^{-2} ruptures per second, 6 of 10 4 mm devices pass G1 and the pooled criterion fails. The 3-device pass of the last report was again luck of the first devices; by the rule fixed in advance the 4 mm claim is withdrawn.
  • The pass rate does not depend on size. Over 1, 2, 2.83 and 4 mm, 8, 8, 9 and 6 of 10 devices pass (31 of 40). From 2 mm on, memory and nonlinear capacity pass in every device; the failures are all on NARMA-10, whose error sits just below the 0.70 threshold (0.56-0.66 on average) with a run-to-run spread of about 0.1.
  • Edge electrodes (split bands) give a weaker reservoir than the pad array: about 30 % less capacity without noise, 15 % less at realistic noise, fewer devices passing G1 (5-7 of 10).
  • But they are more robust to ruptures, and more so the larger the network: they keep 63 % of their capacity at 2.83 mm (pads 49 %) and almost never collapse (1 of 20 runs, pads 10 of 20).
  • Bias + small input does not help the edge layout under ruptures (all runs collapse at 10−210^{-2} per second).

Pads: the 4×44 \times 4 array of the previous reports (8 driven, 8 grounded, inside the network). Split bands: two edge strips (5 % of the side) cut into 8 driven segments on the left and 8 grounded segments on the right. Readout = the 16 electrode currents in both. Each device: 2 independent streams of 800 inputs at 100 ms per input. The analysis and the decision rules were written down before the runs (docs/PLAN_N10L_SEEDS.md, docs/PLAN_SPLIT_BANDS.md).

Why: between edge strips the current spreads over many long parallel paths, so a single rupture changes little; between neighbouring pads the paths are few and short and one rupture can reroute much of the current. The same long paths give the edge layout less memory and nonlinearity to start with, and the robustness does not make up for it.

Bias + small input on split bands (the operating point of the measured devices of Milano et al. 2025): without noise it raises capacity by about 40 % (MC 3.2, degree-2 7.2), still well below the biased pads (5.8, 10.9); with ruptures every run collapses, as with the pads.

A side finding. For the pads, an input window at 1.5 ×\times the switching onset (beyond the two windows used so far) gives more capacity, also with ruptures (3 devices: MC 2.05, degree-2 3.36, NARMA 0.61, all 3 passing). The pad results so far may therefore be slightly conservative. For the split bands it changes nothing.

What this means

  • The size claim narrows: at a realistic junction noise level, about 3 of 4 simulated devices of 1-4 mm compute beyond their input, at about half their noise-free capacity. Size makes the devices more alike in capacity but does not raise the share that pass; NARMA-10 decides.
  • Edge electrodes, the easiest to fabricate, cost capacity; their tolerance to ruptures is better and improves with size, which matters for centimetre devices.
  • Ruptures remain the limiting physics for both layouts.
