# Plan N11: calibrating the junction model on measured AgNW data (Milano et al. 2025)

*Written 2026-10-13, before any implementation or fit. The targets, losses and decision rules below are fixed in
advance; changes after the first data must be logged here with their date and reason.*

## 1. Why

Every noise result so far (N10-R to N10-SB) rests on two hand-set choices in the N10 junction:

* the voltage scale `v_scale` = 5, while N10-C found that matching the measured onset needs about 30-60 (N10 switches at
  about 12 $\times$ the voltage of the measured network);
* random ruptures at a **constant** rate per junction, the same for every junction whatever its state.

The decisive number, the real per-junction rupture rate $r^\ast \approx 0.005\text{–}0.03$ /s (N10-C), came from a long size
extrapolation (gap / wire length 14-40 $\to$ 200) whose largest point had few jumps. N11 replaces these choices by a fit
to the measured data, lets the data choose between a constant and a state-dependent rupture law, and removes the size
extrapolation by simulating the measured gap directly. Then the main reservoir tests are repeated with the fitted
junction (Juan, 2026-10-13: "both, as one calibration").

## 2. Data: what is measured and what is model

Milano, Michieletti, Pilati, Ricciardi, Miranda 2025, *Nat. Commun.*, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-58741-2; dataset Zenodo
10.5281/zenodo.15050217 (CC-BY), in `data/milano2025/`. Ag nanowires 20-50 µm long, about 115 nm diameter, drop-cast on
about 1 $\times$ 1 cm SiO$_2$; two facing Au electrodes about 7 mm apart; two-terminal; sampling about 1.6 Hz; apparently
one device. The paper is mainly a modelling paper (network-level Ornstein-Uhlenbeck model with Poisson jumps) built on
these measurements; its reservoir benchmarks are simulations. Checked against the captions:

| measured (fit targets) | their model (cross-check only) |
|---|---|
| Fig. 1d-f: potentiation at 1 V, relaxation at 10 mV | Fig. 2c; Fig. 2d-g (fitted $\tilde g$, $\theta$, $\kappa_P$, $\kappa_D$ vs voltage) |
| Fig. 2a: transients, 80 s, at 66 voltages 0.1-6.6 V | Fig. 4 (model version of Fig. 3) |
| Fig. 2b: steady state vs voltage (data points; the line is their fit) | Figs. 5b-c, 6, 7, 8, 9 |

**Limits.** One device; network level only (no junction is observed); about 1.6 Hz sampling, so switching faster than
about 1 s is not resolved; the Fig. 2b inset is spaced 0.3 s, finer than 1.6 Hz, and may be resampled (checked in
stage 0 before any jump is counted).

## 3. Geometry: a strip with the measured gap

* **Strip.** A rectangle of length $L_x$ (electrode to electrode) and width $L_y$, electrodes at the two short ends over
  the full width (bands of 5 % of $L_x$, as the framework's `band_frac`). Built here (`agnw_rc/strip.py`,
  `make_strip(seed, length_mm, width_mm)`): wires generated by the framework's algorithm generalised to a rectangle
  (uniform centres, uniform angles, 70 µm long, 2000 wires per mm$^2$), junctions from the framework's
  `find_intersections`, circuit and dual-node solver unchanged. **Exactness:** $L_x = L_y$ reproduces
  `agnw_sim.geometry.build_network` bit for bit.
* **Length.** The number of wire lengths across the gap sets the number of junctions in series and hence the onset
  (N10-C scaled with gap / wire length). Measured: $7\ \mathrm{mm} / 35\ \mathrm{\mu m} = 200$; with our 70 µm wires the
  main strip is **14 mm**. A 7 mm strip (gap / wire length 100) runs as a check.
* **Width.** 0.5 mm (about 7 wire lengths), about 42 000 junctions. Checked in stage 1 at 0.25 and 1 mm: onset and
  normalised steady state must agree within 5 %; otherwise the width is raised.
* **Density.** Our 9.8 ($n L^2$), not their mass-based estimate of about 5.8: at 5.8 small networks barely conduct and single
  ruptures disconnect them (N10-C), which the data never show. Stated as a limitation; `v_scale` absorbs part of it.

## 4. Junction model changes (`agnw_rc/volatile.py`)

Random ruptures of the volatile filament $x_j$ (a fraction `rupture_frac` of $x_j$ is lost at a rupture) happen at a rate
per junction

$$
r_j = \begin{cases} r_0 & \text{constant (current N10)} \\ r_0\,(P_j/P_0)^{\alpha} & \text{power law} \\ r_0\,e^{P_j/P^\ast} & \text{thermally activated} \end{cases}
$$

with $P_j = G_j V_j^2$ the junction power (already computed for the deterministic rupture above $P_c$) and $P_0$ a fixed
reference: the median junction power of the 14 mm strip at 1.5 $\times$ its onset, so that $r_0$ stays comparable with the
current constant rate. New `VolatileParams` fields: `rupture_law`, `rupture_alpha`, `rupture_P0`, `rupture_Pstar`;
defaults reproduce the current model.

**Fitted:** `v_scale`, median $\tau_s$, spread $\sigma_\mathrm{ln}$, $k_\mathrm{max}$ (stage 1); rupture law, $r_0$, $\alpha$ or $P^\ast$,
`rupture_frac`, Gaussian junction noise $\sigma$ (stage 2). **Fixed:** the Milano 2022 $\eta_p$, $\eta_d$ (their voltage
dependence enters through `v_scale`), $G_\mathrm{on}$, $G_\mathrm{off}$, the deterministic power rupture; consolidation off
($k_c = 0$): the stationary records cannot separate it.

## 5. Stage 0: summary statistics of the measured data

`scripts/n11_data.py` $\to$ `results/n11_data.json`, `figures/fig33_n11_data.png`. The same functions are later applied to
simulated conductance sampled at the measured times.

| statistic | source | estimator |
|---|---|---|
| onset $V_\mathrm{on}$ | Fig. 2b points | voltage where the steady state reaches 10 $\times$ the low-voltage value (N10-C definition) |
| steady-state shape | Fig. 2b points | $G(V)/G(6.6\ \mathrm{V})$ for $V > V_\mathrm{on}$ (absolute siemens depend on the width) |
| potentiation rate $\theta(V)$ | Fig. 2a | least-squares exponential approach to the plateau, per voltage |
| relaxation after a pulse | Fig. 1e | decay time(s); one vs two exponentials compared |
| noise sd, jump rate $\lambda(V)$, up/down share, jump sizes | Fig. 2b inset | detrend; $\Delta G/G$ per sample; robust sd (MAD); jump = $\lvert \Delta G/G \rvert$ above 2.6 robust sd |
| anchor at 3.6 V | Figs. 3, 5a | the N10-C values (0.080 jumps /s, size exponent about 3) |

Uncertainties: bootstrap over blocks of the traces (block 10 s); Poisson intervals for jump counts. Only voltages above
the onset enter the jump statistics (about 40 jumps per 500 s each). Stage 0 reports whether $\lambda$ rises much faster with
voltage than the conducting fraction (a first hint of a state-dependent law); the decision is made in stage 2.

**Estimator validation (before use):** on synthetic series (exponential relaxation + Gaussian noise + Poisson jumps with
power-law sizes, known parameters, sampled like the data) the estimators must recover $\lambda$ within 15 %, the sd within
10 % and $\theta$ within 20 %.

## 6. Stage 1: deterministic fit (junction noise off)

1. **`v_scale` from the onset alone:** ramp sweeps (as N10-C / N10-L) on the 14 mm strip, 1-D root find of
   onset($v_\mathrm{scale}$) $= V_\mathrm{on}$ measured; network seeds 1-2. Width check (0.25 / 0.5 / 1 mm) and the 7 mm strip at
   the result.
2. **$\tau_s$, $\sigma_\mathrm{ln}$, $k_\mathrm{max}$:** $3 \times 3 \times 3$ grid (initial ranges $\tau_s$ 0.3 / 3 / 30 s, $\sigma_\mathrm{ln}$ 0 / 1 / 2,
   $k_\mathrm{max}$ 10 / 100 / 1000 /s, re-centred if stage 0 places the measured relaxation outside), at 8 voltages spanning
   onset to 6.6 V, then one refinement around the best point.
3. **Loss:** $\sum_k \big(\log s_k^\mathrm{sim} - \log s_k^\mathrm{data}\big)^2 / \delta_k^2$ over the steady-state shape points, $\theta(V)$ and
   the relaxation time(s), with $\delta_k$ the bootstrap uncertainty of the logged data statistic (floor 0.05).

If $\tau_s$ ends far above 0.3 s, washouts ($\ge 3\,\tau_s$) and holds grow: stage 1 re-estimates the budget of stages 2-3
before they are launched. The 2 ms step is re-checked against 1 ms at the fitted parameters (stationary $G$ and $\theta$
within 2 %).

## 7. Stage 2: noise fit (stage-1 parameters fixed)

* **Candidates:** constant ($r_0$), power law ($r_0$, $\alpha$), thermal ($r_0$, $P^\ast$); each with `rupture_frac` $\in$
  {1, 0.5} and Gaussian junction noise $\sigma$.
* **Simulation:** stationary state at 6 measured voltages above onset (those with $\ge 20$ detected jumps, spread evenly
  over the range), washout then 500 s, sampled like the data, network seeds
  1-2; coarse grid ($r_0$ over 4 decades, $\alpha \in$ {0.5, 1, 2}, $P^\ast$ at 3 values around the junction-power spread),
  then refinement around each law's best point.
* **Targets:** $\lambda(V)$, sd($V$), jump-size distribution (largest jump, share above 1 % of $G$), up/down share.
* **Loss:** Poisson deviance of the jump counts per voltage + $\sum (\log \mathrm{sd}^\mathrm{sim} - \log \mathrm{sd}^\mathrm{data})^2/\delta^2$ +
  10 $\times$ the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance of the pooled jump sizes (deviance and squared errors are on the
  $-2 \log L$ scale). Information criterion $\mathrm{AIC} = \text{loss} + 2k$, $k$ = number of fitted parameters.
* **Choice of law (fixed now):** a state-dependent law (power or thermal) replaces the constant rate only if its AIC
  is lower by more than 4 **and** its $\lambda(V)$ lies inside the 95 % data intervals at the voltages where the constant
  law's does not. Otherwise the constant law stays.
* **Output:** the chosen law with a 95 % interval for $r_0$ (profile of the loss, $\Delta \mathrm{AIC} \le 4$), and the mean rate
  per junction it implies at the reservoir operating point, taken from the model directly, **without size
  extrapolation**. Compared with N10-C ($0.005\text{–}0.03$ /s).

## 8. Stage 3: the reservoir with the fitted junction

* **N11-E (no noise):** pads, 1 mm, networks 1-10; input windows from a new onset sweep (1.05 / 1.25 $\times$ onset, width
  0.45 $\times$ onset, as N10-LS); hold time chosen on validation among $\{\tau_s/3,\ \tau_s\}$ if $\tau_s$ moved from 0.3 s,
  otherwise 100 ms; 800 inputs, 50 virtual nodes, 2 i.i.d. replicates + 1 sine/square.
* **N11-R (fitted noise):** pads at 1 and 2.83 mm, networks 1-10, the fitted law at its best fit and at both ends of its
  interval; rupture-free twins for $\rho$; plus a rupture ladder (networks 1-3, 1 mm) for the tolerance $r_{75}$. With a
  state-dependent law the effective mean rate per junction is reported.
* Same analysis code as N10-LS (`n10l_seeds.analyse`): pooled G0-G2, networks passing G1 (Clopper-Pearson), spread, $\rho$,
  collapses.

## 9. Decision rules (fixed before any fit)

1. **Calibration passes** if the fitted model has the onset within $\pm 10\%$ of the measured one, an RMS log-error of the
   steady-state shape below 0.2, and $\theta(V)$ within a factor 2 at $\ge 75\%$ of the fitted voltages. If not, the
   result is "the N10 form cannot reproduce the measured device" (reported as such); stage 3 still runs on the best fit,
   flagged.
2. **Rupture law:** section 7 rule.
3. **Reservoir verdict** (N10-LS rules unchanged): claim "computes beyond its input at fitted noise" with pooled G1 and
   $\ge 9/10$ networks; "most devices" if pooled fails and $\ge 7/10$; failed below $7/10$.
4. **Manuscript:** if N11-R at 2.83 mm meets rule 3's claim at the best-fit noise, thesis A ("ruptures are the limit")
   is revisited with Juan; otherwise thesis A stands, now on a fitted, measured-data basis.

## 12. Changes after writing (log)

* **2026-10-13 (implementation plan, before any fit):** stage-0 estimators made precise.
  1. All inset voltages are reduced to a 0.6 s grid (nearest sample), because the inset is sampled at 0.1-0.6 s with
     repeated time stamps.
  2. The Gaussian sd is a sigma-clipped estimate. A plain MAD reads 6-25 % high with data-like jumps.
  3. The expected Gaussian false positives, $N\,2\Phi(-2.6)$, are subtracted from the event count. They are about
     20 % of the measured rate.
  4. Validation: $\lambda$ within 15 % applies to jumps clearly above the threshold ($\ge 5$ sd). For power-law sizes
     starting at the threshold, the detection efficiency is reported, not tested. Simulated series go through the
     same estimator.
  5. The onset is redefined as $G = 10^{-3}\,G_\mathrm{sat}$ (median $G$ at $V \ge 5$ V), log-interpolated, because
     "10 $\times$ the low-voltage value" is ill-defined at the instrument floor. The shape is normalised by
     $G_\mathrm{sat}$. Stage 1 uses the same definitions on simulated ramps.
* **2026-10-13 (stage 0 run):** the Fig. 5a anchor uses 18 500-35 600 s, because the record falls to the instrument
  floor at 35 653 s (bias off).
* **2026-10-13 (final review of part 1):** inset records whose mean differs by more than 10 % from the Fig. 2b point
  are not stationary and are excluded from the jump and noise statistics (2.6 and 2.7 V). The shape target is the
  Fig. 2b points, as section 5 says. A transient with no plateau in 80 s (fitted $\theta$ below 1 / record length, or
  plateau above 3 $\times$ the largest $G$) is flagged unresolved. The $\lambda$ trend is reported with its standard
  error.
* **2026-10-13 (part 2 implementation plan, docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-13-n11-part2-fit.md, before any fit):**
  1. The strip is **15.6 mm** long. The electrode bands take 5 % of $L_x$ each, so the free gap is 14.0 mm, which is
     200 wire lengths as measured. The check strip is 7.8 mm.
  2. `v_scale` follows $\tau_s$. The junction threshold $k_p = k_d$ sits at
     $V_j^\ast = \ln(1/(k_{p0}\tau_s)) / ((\eta_p + \eta_d)\,v_\mathrm{scale})$, so `v_scale` is rescaled by
     $\ln(1/(k_{p0}\tau_s))$ from the root find at the reference point ($\tau_s$ 0.3 s, spread 1, $k_\mathrm{max}$ 100 /s).
     The onset stays in the loss and in rule 1.
  3. Stage 1 uses 11 voltages (2.0-6.6 V), so that some lie below the onset, as needed to locate it. The grid runs on
     network seed 1, and seed 2 is run at the best point.
  4. Each run starts from a relaxed device, as in Figs. 2a and 5a, and lasts $\mathrm{clip}(20\,\tau_s, 200, 1000)$ s.
     $\theta$ is fitted on the first 80 s. The steady state is the mean of the last 100 s, flagged unconverged if it
     differs by more than 2 % from the 100 s before it.
  5. The $\theta$ targets are smoothed: the median of the resolved values within $\pm 0.3$ V, with $\delta$ including
     the neighbours' log-sd. The measured $\theta$ scatters about 10-fold between neighbouring voltages.
  6. The relaxation target is the one-exponential time, 56 s, with $\delta = \ln(56/27)/2$. Simulated relaxation:
     drive at 3.7 V, then 10 mV for 250 s.
  7. $P_0$ is the median power of the formed junctions ($x > 0.5$) in the best stage-1 run at 3.6 V.
  8. Stage 2 starts from the deterministic steady state, then runs a 100 s noisy washout and the 500 s record.
     $\sigma = 0$ on the coarse grid; it is added only if the best law's sd is below half the measured sd.
  9. The Poisson deviance uses raw event counts, with the simulated count scaled to the data duration and the mean
     floored at 0.5. The parameter counts are $k$ = 2 (constant) and 3 (power, thermal).
